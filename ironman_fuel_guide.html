Ironman Fuel Guide

Session fuelling + race day nutrition — based on your weight, in real food.

A guide, not a prescription. These recommendations are based on evidence-based sports nutrition guidelines and are designed to support your training. Always adjust based on how you feel, listen to your body, and work with your coach on session specifics. If you have any medical conditions or dietary needs beyond gluten free, speak to a registered dietitian.

Your Ironman will take 8–16 hours. Nutrition is the fourth discipline — plan it, practice it in training, and never try anything new on race day.

Golden rule: Everything here should be practiced in training first. Your gut needs to learn to absorb carbs while you're moving. Start now — not race week.
3 days out — carb loading begins
Increase carbs to 8–10 g/kg per day
You don't need to deplete first — just increase. Full glycogen stores can be achieved in 1–3 days of high carb intake combined with reduced training (taper).
🍝
Large pasta meals
White pasta + simple tomato sauce + chicken. Twice a day if needed. Keep fat low.
~80–100g carbs per bowl
🍚
White rice with every meal
White rice digests faster than brown — better for loading. 250g cooked portions.
~55g carbs per portion
🥯
Bagels, white bread, bananas as snacks
Keep snacking on fast carbs between meals. Fruit juice is fine here.
30–55g carbs per snack
🥔
Jacket potatoes
Easy, familiar, carb-dense. With a small amount of protein — not butter-heavy.
~50g carbs
Keep fat and fibre low during these 3 days. High fibre veg, salads, and fatty foods slow digestion and take up stomach space you need for carbs. This is not the week for a big salad.

GF note: Use GF pasta, GF bread, and rice as your main sources. Check all labels on sports products.
Night before
Simple carb dinner — nothing new
Familiar food only. This is not the night to try a new restaurant or a new recipe.
🍝
Pasta + simple tomato sauce + chicken or fish
No creamy sauces, no salad, no alcohol. Large portion of pasta, moderate protein.
~90g carbs
🍞
Bread roll on the side
White roll, a little butter is fine. Extra carbs without thinking about it.
~25g carbs
Sleep is nutrition. Get to bed early. A poor night's sleep the night before a race is normal and won't ruin performance — but you want to be rested going into it. Aim for 9pm.
Race morning — 2–3 hours before start
1–4 g/kg carbs — low fat, low fibre, familiar
This is the most important meal of the day. Stick to what you've practiced in training. No experimenting.
🍞
White toast + peanut butter + banana
2–3 slices white toast, thin spread of PB, 1 banana. Simple and proven.
~75–90g carbs
🥣
Or: porridge + banana + honey
80g oats (GF if needed) + banana + tbsp honey. Warm, comforting, easy to eat when nervous.
~85g carbs
☕
Black coffee (optional)
If you train with coffee, have it now. If you don't normally, don't start on race day.
Hydration: Drink 500ml water with breakfast. Then 5–10 ml/kg fluid in the 2 hours before start. Aim for pale yellow urine before you get in the water.

15 mins before start: 1 gel + 200–300ml water. Tops up blood glucose right as the gun goes.
The swim — ~1–1.5 hours
Water only — no fuel needed in the water
You can't eat in the swim and you don't need to. Focus on pace and staying calm. Your pre-race carbs are doing the work.
Get out of the swim feeling in control. The bike is where the nutrition work starts. Take a moment in T1 to get a gel in before you mount the bike.
The bike — 180km, ~5–8 hours
60–90 g carbs per hour — start eating early
The bike is your kitchen. You have time and opportunity to eat. Don't wait until you're hungry — by then it's too late. Start fuelling in the first 30 minutes.
🏅
Gels — 1 every 30–45 mins
Glucose + fructose gels absorb faster (up to 90g/hr). Wash down with water, not sports drink — mixing sugars is fine, mixing liquids causes GI issues.
~22g carbs per gel
🥤
Electrolyte drink on the bike
Don't drink plain water only. Mix with electrolyte tabs or use an isotonic drink. Sodium prevents hyponatraemia on long efforts in heat.
30–35g carbs per 500ml
🍌
Real food in the first half of the bike
Banana, rice cakes, dates, or a soft energy bar. Easier on the gut early. Stomach gets more sensitive later in the race.
25–35g carbs
🍬
Chews or blocks as an alternative to gels
Some people find chewing easier than gels on the bike. Practice with whatever you plan to use on race day.
~25g carbs per serving
Caffeine strategy: Save your caffeine gels for the second half of the bike and the run. A 200mg caffeine hit around hour 3–5 of the bike is well-timed. Don't front-load caffeine — you'll need it more later.

GF reminder: Check every gel, chew, and sports drink label. Many contain maltodextrin from wheat — not all are GF. Know your products before race day.
The run — 42km, ~4–6 hours
30–60 g carbs per hour — gut gets harder here
The run is where nutrition falls apart for most people. Your gut has been working for 6+ hours. Keep it simple, keep it liquid if you can, and use the aid stations.
🏅
Gels — but slow down the rate slightly
1 gel every 40–60 mins on the run. Your gut may struggle to absorb as much as on the bike. Listen to it.
~22g carbs per gel
🥤
Flat cola from aid stations
From about 30km onwards, flat cola is one of the best things you can take. Fast carbs, small amount of caffeine, settles the stomach. A race day classic.
~26g carbs per 250ml
🍗
Chicken broth / savoury options at aid stations
If you're feeling nauseous of sweet things (very common late in an Ironman), savoury broth provides sodium and a mental reset. Take it.
🍌
Banana halves at aid stations
Easy to grab, easy to eat, familiar. Don't overthink it — if it's there and you feel okay, take it.
~15g carbs
If GI issues hit: Back off the gels, switch to cola and broth, keep sipping electrolytes. Walk if you need to — it's better to walk for 5 minutes and recover than to blow up completely.

Hydration on the run: Drink at every aid station. Small sips, don't gulp. Pouring water over your head helps with core temperature in heat — keep drinking for internal hydration separately.
After the finish line
Recovery starts now — but gently
Your gut has been through a lot. Don't force food immediately. Start with fluids and small amounts.
🥤
Electrolytes first
Rehydrate before you eat. Coconut water, electrolyte drink, or just water with a pinch of salt.
🍫
Chocolate milk or recovery shake when ready
When your stomach settles — 30–60 mins post finish. Protein + carbs to start the recovery process.
~20–25g protein
🍕
Eat whatever you want tonight
Seriously. You just finished an Ironman. Have the pizza, have the burger. Your body will deal with it. Celebrate.
The days after: Don't restrict. Reverse out of the deficit and eat to maintenance or just above. Your body needs to repair — protein stays high, carbs come back in. Sleep as much as you can.
Products to check if you're gluten free: Always check the label on gels, chews, energy bars, and sports drinks before race day. Brands change formulations. Know exactly what you're using and carry your own if you can't guarantee what's available on course.
